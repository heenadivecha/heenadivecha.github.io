About

I am a third-year PhD candidate in the Solomon H. Snyder Department of Neuroscience at Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, carrying out my research at the Lieber Institute for Brain Development.

I study the locus coeruleus (LC), a small brainstem nucleus that supplies most of the brain’s norepinephrine and is one of the first regions to accumulate tau pathology in Alzheimer’s disease. Combining spatial transcriptomics with protein and neuromelanin imaging in postmortem human brain, I map how aging, neuromelanin and phosphorylated tau relate to changes in LC neurons and their glial and vascular neighbors, to understand why the LC is vulnerable so early in disease.
